# Long Beach Living Breakwater Retrofit

**A preliminary concept for combining coastal infrastructure with marine habitat restoration in Long Beach, California.**

## Project summary

This project explores whether selected sections of the existing Long Beach Breakwater could be retrofitted with habitat-enhancing surfaces while retaining the structure's primary coastal-protection function. The concept is inspired by habitat-enhancing breakwater approaches investigated in Okinawa, adapted for Southern California's marine ecology and local engineering constraints.

The initial approach is conservative: retain the existing protective profile and evaluate replaceable or mechanically secured habitat modules at carefully selected locations. Submerged reef additions are a separate option and should be considered only after site-specific analysis.

## Goals

- Preserve structural integrity, harbor protection, and navigation safety.
- Increase suitable hard-substrate habitat and ecological complexity for native Southern California species.
- Evaluate effects on wave transmission, overtopping, currents, scour, sediment transport, and water circulation.
- Establish a measurable, staged pilot with control sites and long-term monitoring.
- Build on, rather than duplicate, prior Long Beach and U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) ecosystem-restoration work.

## Preliminary design elements

1. **Habitat modules:** marine-compatible textured surfaces, cavities, and crevices, installed only where structural analysis permits.
2. **Intertidal and submerged zones:** different geometries suited to exposure, depth, light, and flow.
3. **Optional reef-toe elements:** considered only where bathymetry, foundation conditions, sediment behavior, and ecological review support them.
4. **Monitoring:** baseline and post-installation measurements of structural condition, hydraulics, water quality, and native biological communities.

## Proposed workflow

1. Review existing studies, as-built drawings, inspections, bathymetry, wave/current records, and habitat surveys.
2. Select candidate locations and document constraints.
3. Compare the baseline with habitat-only and habitat-plus-reef alternatives.
4. Complete preliminary structural, hydrodynamic, sediment, and ecological assessments.
5. Conduct laboratory or physical-model testing where warranted.
6. Seek agency and regulatory review before any field installation.
7. Install a limited pilot and monitor it across multiple seasons.
8. Expand only if predefined safety and ecological criteria are met.

## Important limitations

This repository documents a **concept for investigation**, not a final engineering design or a claim of demonstrated performance. No site survey, structural verification, hydraulic modeling, environmental review, permitting, or cost estimate has been completed as part of this concept. It does not assert that the retrofit would improve tsunami protection or reduce flooding. Those outcomes would require independent, site-specific evidence.

All dimensions, material specifications, attachment systems, costs, and performance targets remain to be established by qualified coastal, structural, geotechnical, and ecological professionals.
