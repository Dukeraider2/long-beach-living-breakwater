## Okinawa precedent

1. **Tanaya, T., Kinjo, N., Okada, W., Yasuda, M., & Kuwae, T. (2021).** “Improvement of the coral growth and cost-effectiveness of hybrid infrastructure by an innovative breakwater design in Naha Port, Okinawa, Japan.” *Coastal Engineering Journal*, 63(3), 248–262. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/21664250.2021.1889823  
   - Publisher full text: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21664250.2021.1889823  
   - Publisher PDF: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/21664250.2021.1889823  
   - Abstract and metadata: https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1883961343048087808  
   - Relevance: compares a control breakwater with pro-environment designs using artificial tide pools at different depths; reports coral-area and construction-cost comparisons. The findings are specific to the studied Okinawa context and do not predict Long Beach results.

## Evidence handling

For each source, record the title, issuing organization, publication date, URL, retrieval date, relevant page/section, and the claim it supports. Prefer primary agency reports and peer-reviewed research. Clearly distinguish source findings from hypotheses and assumptions.

Do not transfer Okinawan coral percentages, tropical species assumptions, or study-specific cost-effectiveness figures to Long Beach. For the Naha study, the abstract reports roughly 10% and 20% greater coral area for shallow and deeper artificial-tide-pool designs, while detailed results report approximately 14% and 22% greater coral area per meter, respectively. Its cost-effectiveness metric was coral area divided by construction cost, not a complete lifecycle-cost or coastal-protection analysis.
