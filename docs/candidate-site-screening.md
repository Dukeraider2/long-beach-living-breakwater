# Candidate-Site Screening: Long Beach Living Breakwater Retrofit

**Status:** Desktop screening framework only. No installation site is selected or approved.

## 1. Purpose

Identify where a habitat-enhancing retrofit could be studied without compromising the Long Beach Breakwater's coastal-protection function, port operations, navigation, public safety, or existing ecological values. This is a screening document, not a site survey or engineering recommendation.

The central question is narrower than “where can habitat be added?”: **where could a small, reversible or replaceable habitat treatment be tested on the existing structure, if the owner and engineers determine that the structure can safely accept it?**

## 2. Geographic context

The USACE East San Pedro Bay Ecosystem Restoration study covers waters between the Long Beach shoreline and the offshore Middle and Long Beach Breakwaters. Its planning work considered rocky reefs, kelp reefs, eelgrass, wetlands, and other habitat measures over a much broader area than a retrofit to the existing breakwater. It is therefore a valuable regional baseline, but its recommended restoration plan should not be treated as approval for attaching modules to the breakwater.

Sources:
- [USACE East San Pedro Bay Ecosystem Restoration Study: final report and appendices](https://www.spl.usace.army.mil/Missions/Civil-Works/Projects-and-Studies/East-San-Pedro-Bay-Ecosystem-Restoration-Study/)
- [USACE project fact sheet, April 16, 2023](https://www.spl.usace.army.mil/Media/Fact-Sheets/Article/3364187/investigations-east-san-pedro-bay-ecosystem-restoration-ca/)
- [City of Long Beach Bay Ecosystem Study](https://www.longbeach.gov/citymanager/tidelands/bay-ecosystem-study/)

The USACE fact sheet lists financial closeout in FY2023 and no planned accomplishments for FY2024. That historical status is not confirmation of current implementation or funding. Confirm the latest status with USACE and the City before aligning this concept with any active project.

## 3. Screening zones to compare

These are **hypothesis categories**, not named or mapped installation locations. They should be translated into chainage-referenced segments only after the owner provides current drawings, condition records, access constraints, and geospatial data.

| Screening zone | Why examine it | Principal disqualifiers / questions |
|---|---|---|
| Sheltered, harbor-facing slope or toe | Potentially lower wave exposure than the seaward face; may support a conservative habitat-surface concept | Does attachment load or toe work affect armor stability, foundation, circulation, water quality, or maintenance access? What are actual local velocities and scour conditions? |
| Submerged, low-interference section away from gates and navigation approaches | Could permit a controlled test below the most heavily used surface zone | Is there suitable depth, substrate, light, water quality, and access? Would modules interfere with vessel clearance, anchoring, fisheries, or existing habitat? |
| Existing disturbed or low-complexity surface identified by survey | May reduce the risk of replacing valuable existing habitat | Requires biological baseline and seasonal survey; “disturbed” must be demonstrated, not assumed |
| Exposed seaward face | Ecologically interesting because exposure and flow may create different habitat conditions | Highest concern for wave loading, attachment fatigue, access, storm damage, and consequences of detachment; defer unless engineering evidence supports study |

### Initial exclusions

Do not nominate a pilot segment within or immediately adjacent to:
- Queen's Gate, other navigation channels, or port approach/turning areas;
- vessel berths, operational port infrastructure, or maintenance access routes;
- locations where a module could detach and become a navigation hazard;
- known sensitive habitat or protected-species areas until agencies review them;
- unstable armor, active scour, damaged structure, or areas without verified structural capacity;
- locations with unresolved conflicts with existing restoration plans, public access, fishing, or recreation.

These are preliminary screening rules. The relevant owner and agencies must define actual buffers and exclusion boundaries.

## 4. Data needed before selecting a segment

Request and version-control the following data. Record the source, date, coordinate system, vertical datum, resolution, limitations, and permission to use each dataset.

### Structure and geotechnical
- Original design and as-built drawings, including breakwater cross-sections, armor size/gradation, crest and toe elevations, filter layers, core, and foundation.
- Owner inspection records, repairs, settlement, armor displacement, storm damage, and maintenance history.
- Geotechnical and foundation information, including known scour and toe instability.
- Permissible additional loads, allowable drilling/anchoring methods, and any restrictions on modifying the existing structure.

### Coastal processes and navigation
- Recent multibeam bathymetry and side-scan/backscatter data around candidate segments.
- Wave climate, water levels, storm conditions, overtopping and wave-transmission records or models.
- Current measurements or validated current fields, sediment transport and scour assessments.
- Current official nautical charts, vessel routes, channel boundaries, anchorage information, and operational access constraints.

NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information provides a [Bathymetric Data Viewer](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/maps/bathymetry/). NOAA hydrographic survey metadata for Southern California approaches to Long Beach include survey H13198, acquired in 2018. These are useful discovery leads, **not a substitute for current, fit-for-purpose bathymetry or official navigation products**. Check coverage and resolution before use. See [NOAA survey H13198](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/nos/H12001-H14000/H13198.html).

### Ecology and water quality
- Seasonal biological surveys of existing hard substrate, soft bottom, kelp, eelgrass, invertebrates, fish, and protected species.
- Water quality, turbidity, light availability, temperature, dissolved oxygen, and contamination information relevant to the candidate depth and location.
- Existing restoration project footprints, monitoring data, and agency-designated sensitive areas.
- A clear reference/control-site plan so natural seasonal variability can be distinguished from treatment effects.

### Ownership and approvals
- Confirm ownership, asset operator, maintenance responsibility, and authority to authorize testing.
- Identify federal, state, local, and port approvals that may apply.
- Confirm consistency with current USACE and City restoration plans and any environmental documents.
- Establish who owns and maintains the modules, who inspects them after storms, and who is responsible if a component moves or fails.

## 5. Transparent scoring framework

After the data above are available, score each candidate segment from 0 to 5 against documented evidence. A missing dataset is **unknown**, not a score of zero and not a pass.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence needed |
|---|---:|---|
| Structural compatibility and low consequence of failure | 25% | Verified structure records, engineer review, attachment/load analysis |
| Navigation and operational compatibility | 20% | Port/USACE review, chart and vessel-use constraints |
| Ecological suitability and additionality | 20% | Seasonal baseline, habitat mapping, control sites |
| Wave/current/scour suitability | 15% | Bathymetry, measured or validated hydrodynamics, sediment analysis |
| Access, inspectability, and removability | 10% | Safe access plan, inspection and recovery method |
| Monitoring feasibility and cost | 10% | Baseline design, sensors/survey access, defensible cost estimate |

The weights are provisional decision-support values, not a validated multi-criteria model. A candidate must also pass **hard safety and regulatory gates**. A high weighted score cannot compensate for structural uncertainty, navigation conflict, or an unresolved legal prohibition.

## 6. Recommended decision gates

1. **Gate A - Authority and records:** confirm asset owner/operator, obtain drawings and current condition records, and identify data-sharing permissions.
2. **Gate B - Desktop exclusions:** map navigation, operations, existing restoration footprints, sensitive habitats, and known geotechnical constraints.
3. **Gate C - Field verification:** complete bathymetric, structural, ecological, and water-quality surveys at the short-listed locations.
4. **Gate D - Engineering and environmental review:** analyze loads, attachment, failure consequences, hydraulics, sediment, and ecological impacts; confirm required approvals.
5. **Gate E - Pilot authorization:** approve a small monitored test only after independent review, baseline monitoring, storm inspection criteria, and a removal contingency are in place.

## 7. Current conclusion

The next defensible action is **not to choose a specific breakwater segment from a map**. It is to obtain the asset records and spatial constraints, then build a GIS screening layer from authoritative data. The first pilot should be selected by verified evidence and owner/agency review, not visual convenience or assumed wave shelter.

No dimensions, coordinates, or installation locations are specified in this document because the necessary site-specific evidence has not yet been verified.
