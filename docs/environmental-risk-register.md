# Preliminary Environmental and Engineering Risk Register

This register is an initial screening aid, not a completed environmental assessment. Likelihood and consequence ratings must be assigned after site-specific data are collected.

| Risk | Potential consequence | Initial mitigation / evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Existing armor destabilized during installation | Reduced breakwater performance or localized failure | Review as-built records; use a structural engineer; develop installation sequence and stop-work criteria |
| Added module or attachment failure | Debris, injury, habitat damage, maintenance burden | Verify design loads, anchorage, material durability, and inspectability; test prototypes |
| Increased local scour | Foundation exposure or nearby sediment changes | Bathymetric baseline; sediment and bed-shear analysis; monitor scour after installation |
| Unwanted wave transmission or overtopping change | Reduced harbor protection or changed shoreline hazards | Calibrated wave modeling; compare baseline and retrofit; reject unacceptable alternatives |
| Sediment deposition in cavities | Reduced habitat value or localized water-quality effects | Test geometry; monitor sediment accumulation; provide maintenance or redesign pathway |
| Sensitive habitat disturbed | Loss or degradation of existing biological communities | Baseline surveys; avoidance buffers; seasonal work restrictions where required |
| Invasive species colonization | Ecological harm or maintenance complications | Monitor species composition; establish response protocol with local ecologists |
| Material leaching or unsuitable surface chemistry | Toxicity or impaired colonization | Review mix/material data; use marine-compatible products; perform relevant leachate testing |
| Turbidity or contaminated sediment resuspension | Water-quality and biological impacts | Sediment characterization; installation controls; monitoring and contingency plan |
| Construction interferes with navigation or port operations | Safety risk, delays, access restrictions | Coordinate with responsible port and navigation authorities; approved work windows and exclusion zones |
| Sea-level rise or changing extremes invalidate assumptions | Reduced long-term performance | Include appropriate future water levels and climate scenarios; document uncertainty |
| Benefits overstated | Poor investment or misleading public claims | Independent review; define baseline and controls; publish methods and limitations |
| Lifecycle maintenance underestimated | Costs exceed expected value | Include inspection, repair, replacement, vessel access, and end-of-life removal in lifecycle estimate |

## Monitoring and response

Before construction, the project team should define:
- Monitoring locations, methods, and frequency
- Structural displacement and scour thresholds that trigger inspection or shutdown
- Water-quality thresholds and reporting procedures
- Ecological indicators and how they will be compared with control sites
- Responsibility and funding for repairs, module removal, and post-project monitoring

No numeric threshold should be invented before applicable standards, baseline variability, and site-specific design conditions are established.
