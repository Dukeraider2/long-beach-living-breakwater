# Preliminary Engineering Concept

## 1. Purpose

Evaluate whether selected portions of the existing Long Beach Breakwater can accommodate habitat-enhancing retrofit elements that support native marine life without materially compromising structural stability, harbor protection, or navigation.

This is a screening-level concept. It is not a design basis, construction specification, or substitute for professional engineering and environmental review.

## 2. Design hypothesis

Okinawa-inspired habitat geometry—such as varied surface relief, attachment surfaces, and protected crevices—may increase ecological opportunities on suitable submerged infrastructure. Whether this is effective in Long Beach depends on local species, depth, light, water motion, sedimentation, grazing, water quality, and existing communities.

The working hypothesis is that **targeted habitat modules can provide measurable ecological benefit while leaving the breakwater's principal protective geometry unchanged**. This must be tested, not assumed.

## 3. Alternatives to compare

### Alternative 0: Existing condition
Document the current structure, hydraulic performance, maintenance needs, habitat, and adjacent environmental conditions.

### Alternative 1: Habitat-only retrofit
Install selected textured modules or compatible surface elements in locations approved by structural analysis. Avoid changes to the overall breakwater cross-section unless explicitly evaluated and approved.

### Alternative 2: Habitat retrofit plus limited reef-toe elements
Evaluate discrete submerged reef units or rock placement in addition to habitat modules. This alternative introduces additional foundation, scour, sediment, and hydraulic questions and should advance only if screening supports it.

## 4. Key engineering workstreams

### Structural and geotechnical
- Obtain as-built drawings, armor-unit details, core and foundation information, inspection history, and maintenance records.
- Determine whether the proposed location can accept additional loads and installation forces.
- Assess wave loading, uplift, module displacement, attachment failure, abrasion, fatigue, settlement, and local scour.
- Confirm installation does not destabilize existing armor or obstruct inspection and maintenance.
- Define design events and applicable standards with the responsible owner and qualified engineers.

### Coastal hydraulics
- Establish baseline wave climate, water levels, currents, and existing wave-transmission performance.
- Compare wave transmission, reflection, overtopping, run-up, and nearshore flow under baseline and retrofit conditions.
- Evaluate storm conditions and relevant sea-level-rise scenarios.
- Use physical-model testing if numerical methods cannot reliably resolve the geometry or loading.

### Sediment and circulation
- Characterize bathymetry, sediment grain size, bed mobility, and nearby shoreline behavior.
- Assess changes in bed shear stress, sediment deposition, erosion, and circulation.
- Check for unintended stagnant zones, sediment trapping, or impacts beyond the pilot footprint.

### Ecology
- Survey baseline benthic and fish communities, sensitive species, invasive species, and nearby kelp, rocky reef, and eelgrass habitat.
- Select target functions with local marine ecologists rather than transplanting Okinawan species assumptions.
- Test material compatibility and potential leaching.
- Establish matched reference locations and measurable monitoring indicators.

### Port operations and constructability
- Coordinate with the breakwater owner, port operators, navigation and security stakeholders, and relevant agencies.
- Evaluate vessel access, construction windows, barge/crane access, worker safety, inspection access, and emergency operations.
- Confirm all required approvals before field work.

## 5. Candidate-site selection

No pilot location is selected in this document. Candidate segments should be ranked using:
- Structural condition and access to reliable records
- Existing habitat value and sensitivity
- Depth, exposure, wave climate, and current conditions
- Foundation and scour susceptibility
- Proximity to navigation routes and port infrastructure
- Sediment contamination and water-quality constraints
- Ability to establish a suitable reference site
- Installation and maintenance costs

Avoid choosing a site solely because it is easy to access; ecological sensitivity and structural suitability are primary constraints.

## 6. Modeling plan

Build and calibrate a baseline representation using measured site data. Compare Alternatives 0, 1, and 2 under representative seasonal conditions and appropriate extreme-event scenarios.

Report at minimum:
- Wave transmission and reflection
- Overtopping and run-up where applicable
- Nearshore current velocity and direction
- Bed shear stress, sediment transport, and local scour
- Loads and stability for modules and existing armor
- Ecological footprint and potential habitat tradeoffs

Document model assumptions, calibration data, uncertainty, sensitivity tests, and independent technical review. Do not claim improved flood or tsunami protection without separate analysis specifically supporting that conclusion.

## 7. Preliminary go/no-go gates

A pilot should proceed only if:
1. Existing records and site investigations are sufficient to support design.
2. Structural and hydraulic analysis finds no unacceptable degradation of protection or stability.
3. Navigation, access, and maintenance requirements can be met.
4. Environmental review identifies a permissible installation method and footprint.
5. A baseline, control design, monitoring plan, and corrective-action process are funded.
6. The pilot has a credible path to removal or repair if performance is unacceptable.

## 8. Deliverables for a feasibility package

- Source and data register
- Site constraints and candidate-segment map
- Existing-condition assessment
- Concept drawings and module alternatives
- Structural and hydraulic modeling plan
- Ecological baseline and monitoring plan
- Environmental risk register
- Permitting and stakeholder matrix
- Lifecycle cost estimate with assumptions and contingency
- Pilot acceptance criteria and stop-work triggers
